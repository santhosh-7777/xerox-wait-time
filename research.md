# Problem
Before lab submissions, students need lab-copy binding and simple xeroxes at the same counter at the same time. The operator gives rough waits ("10 mins", "15 mins"), so students can't know their real wait. They judge it by the crowd instead, and many walk about 300 m to Monte Xerox.

# Person
Parimalnath, operator of the xerox and binding counter located about 30 meters from the CSE building (estimated by walking pace from the building entrance).

# Existing solutions
1. Verbal estimate from the operator ("10 mins", "15 mins"). It is a rough guess with no visible basis, does not change based on how many spiral bindings or large files are ahead, and cannot be checked by waiting students.
2. Monte Xerox, located about 300 meters away (estimated from campus map/walking distance). Students walk there when the counter near CSE looks crowded, which shifts customers away rather than showing how long the closer counter will actually take.

# Sources
1. Queue Psychology: 8 Ways to Make Waits Feel Shorter (ScanQueue blog, updated 14 Jan 2026)
   URL: https://scanqueue.com/blog/customer-queue-psychology
   Note: My main takeaway from this post is that uncertain waits feel much longer to people than a clear, specific time estimate, and that giving a slightly cautious number prevents frustration. This helps explain why vague replies like "10 mins" make students walk away from the counter. Since it is a blog written by a queue-software company, I treat it as a practical summary rather than formal research.

2. David H. Maister, "The Psychology of Waiting Lines" (1985; reprinted 2005)
   URL: https://www.columbia.edu/~ww2040/4615S13/Psychology_of_Waiting_Lines.pdf
   Note: From Maister's paper, I took the idea that unexplained delays and perceived unfairness make waiting feel worse. On earlier visits, I saw a quick 2-page print wait while larger jobs were handled first, and I will check whether this happens during my fieldwork visit. Because the paper relies on everyday observations rather than controlled experiments, I use it as a conceptual guide.
